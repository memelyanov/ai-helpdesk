AI Helpdesk

The problem

The document exists. Nobody can find it.

What the employee types

“how do I get my money back for a taxi to the airport?”

vs.
What the document is called

Business Travel Expense Reimbursement Policy

shared keywords: 0  →  search results: 0

  • Finding the answer requires already knowing which document holds it.
  • Even a correct hit returns a 30-page PDF — the answer is a paragraph, not a file.
  • So people ask a colleague instead, and a self-service question becomes an interruption.
The problem

The idea

Stop searching for a document. Ask for an answer.

01 · INDEX

Upload to a vector database

Parse each .txt / .pdf, split it into overlapping chunks, and store each chunk as an embedding.

02 · RETRIEVE

Search by meaning

The question becomes a vector too. Similarity search returns the closest chunks — no shared keywords required.

03 · ANSWER

Ground the LLM

Those chunks go to the model as context, with one instruction: answer only from this, and cite it.

The hard rule

If retrieval finds nothing relevant, the answer is “I don't have this in the documentation.” A confident invention is worse than no answer.

Why RAG, not fine-tuning

Documents change — re-indexing a file takes seconds, retraining does not. And retrieval can cite its source; model weights cannot.

Walkthrough

What the user actually sees

EmployeeCan I expense a taxi from the airport when I travel for work?
AI Helpdesk Yes. Ground transportation between the airport and your hotel or the office at the destination is reimbursable for approved business travel. Submit the receipt within 30 days of returning. Rides above EUR 80 need your line manager's approval in advance.
Sources — travel-expense-policy.pdf (p. 5) · corporate-card-rules.txt
EmployeeWhich parking spot is assigned to the CEO?
AI Helpdesk I don't have this in the documentation. The corpus covers visitor parking and parking charges as a travel expense, but nothing about assigned spaces.

One turn, one answer, and a citation to verify it — or an honest refusal. The second case is the one worth watching: parking is in the corpus, so retrieval will return something. It still has to say no.

Every fact is genuinely in the named document — the wording a person would reach for is not. A keyword search over this corpus fails most of these, which is the point: if it passed, a good retrieval score would prove nothing about the embeddings. Target: the correct document in the top-K chunks for ≥ 80% of questions.

Scope

What I am proving — and what I am not

In scope

  • Upload and index .txt / .pdf
  • Semantic retrieval over the indexed corpus
  • Grounded answers with source citations
  • Angular chat + upload UI
  • Measured retrieval quality against the CSV

Out of scope

  • Auth and per-user document permissions
  • Real confidential data
  • OCR of scanned PDFs, tables and images
  • Multi-turn conversation memory
  • Visual polish beyond a working chat

Success is five statements, all checkable

A document uploaded is searchable seconds later · the right source lands in the top-K for ≥ 80% of the 20 questions · every answer cites its document · an uncovered question is refused, not invented · end to end under ~5 seconds.  — demo in Week 2.
