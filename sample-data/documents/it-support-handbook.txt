1. SCOPE

This handbook describes how to reach the IT service desk, how requests are
prioritised, what response targets apply, and how equipment is provisioned and
replaced. It covers all Northwind sites and all company-managed endpoints.

It does not cover product infrastructure or customer-facing incidents. Those
follow the incident response plan.


2. HOW TO REACH THE SERVICE DESK

All work must enter through a ticket. Tickets are the only supported channel,
because they carry the audit trail, the asset reference and the approval
record.

  - Self-service portal: the "IT Service Desk" tile on the intranet home page.
    This is the preferred route and covers every request type in section 5.
  - Telephone: extension 4400, staffed 08:00-18:00 CET on business days. Used
    for P1 situations or when the portal itself is unreachable.
  - Walk-up desk: ground floor, 09:00-16:00 on business days.

Requests sent by direct message or personal e-mail to an IT colleague will be
redirected to the portal and will not carry a response target.


3. PRIORITY CLASSIFICATION

Priority is assigned by the service desk on intake, based on business impact
and the number of people affected. Requesters may state an urgency, but they
do not set the priority.

  P1 - Critical
      A whole site, a whole department or a business-critical shared service is
      unable to work. Examples: site-wide network outage, single sign-on
      unavailable, payroll system inaccessible on a pay run day.

  P2 - High
      An individual is unable to perform their role and no workaround exists.
      Examples: notebook or workstation hardware fault, forgotten credentials
      with no self-service reset available, mailbox inaccessible.

  P3 - Medium
      An individual is impaired but can still work using a workaround.
      Examples: intermittent peripheral fault, degraded performance, a
      secondary application failing.

  P4 - Low
      Requests, questions and improvements with no current work stoppage.
      Examples: new equipment requests, software installation, access changes.


4. RESPONSE AND RESOLUTION TARGETS

The acknowledgement target is the time within which a named engineer picks the
ticket up and makes first contact with the requester. It is measured from
ticket creation, not from the moment the requester started having trouble.

  Priority   Acknowledgement          Target resolution
  --------   ----------------------   ---------------------------
  P1         Within 1 hour            Within 4 hours
  P2         Within 4 business hours  Within 1 business day
  P3         Within 1 business day    Within 3 business days
  P4         Within 2 business days   Within 10 business days

Business hours are 08:00-18:00 CET, Monday to Friday, excluding public holidays
at the site where the requester is based. P1 acknowledgement is measured around
the clock and is not limited to business hours.

Where a target is going to be missed, the service desk notifies the requester
before the target expires, with a revised estimate and the reason.


5. EQUIPMENT

5.1 Standard issue

Every employee receives a notebook, a docking station, one external display, a
keyboard and a mouse. This set is issued at the IT service desk during
onboarding and is refreshed on a four-year cycle.

5.2 Additional peripherals

To obtain equipment beyond the standard issue -- an additional display, a
headset, an ergonomic keyboard, a second docking station for a home working
location -- submit an equipment request through the IT service desk portal.

Requests for items on the standard peripherals catalogue are approved by your
line manager and require no further sign-off. The catalogue covers displays,
input devices, headsets, webcams, cable adapters and laptop stands. Delivery is
typically three to five business days from approval.

Anything not on the catalogue, and any single item above EUR 400, additionally
requires department head approval and is reviewed by IT Operations for
compatibility and support cost before it is ordered.

5.3 Faults and replacement

Notebook and workstation hardware faults are handled as P2. Raise a ticket in
the IT service desk portal describing the symptom and the asset tag printed on
the underside of the device.

If the fault prevents you from working and cannot be resolved remotely, the
service desk issues a replacement unit on the next business day. Your profile
and any data synchronised to corporate storage are restored onto the
replacement; anything held only on the local disk and never synchronised
cannot be recovered.

Damaged, lost or stolen equipment must be reported the same day. Loss or theft
additionally requires a report to the security team.

5.4 Returns

Equipment is returned to the IT service desk on the last working day. The
service desk confirms the return in the asset register and releases the final
clearance to People Operations.


6. SOFTWARE

Applications on the approved catalogue can be installed from the self-service
software portal without a ticket. Anything else requires a P4 request and is
assessed for licensing, security and support implications. Employees do not
hold local administrator rights on company-managed endpoints.


7. ACCOUNTS AND ACCESS

Password resets are self-service through the identity portal using a registered
second factor. If no second factor is registered, or the registered device is
unavailable, a P2 ticket is required and identity is verified by video call
with the service desk.

Access to a shared system or a restricted repository is requested through the
portal and approved by the system owner, not by IT. Credential rules are set by
the information security policy.
