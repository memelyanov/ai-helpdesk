NORTHWIND SYSTEMS GMBH
PERFORMANCE REVIEW PROCESS

Document ID: HR-PRF-005
Version: 3.3
Effective date: 15 January 2026
Owner: People Operations
Classification: Internal
Review cycle: Annual


1. PURPOSE

This document describes how contribution and development are assessed at
Northwind Systems: the cadence, the inputs, the calibration step, and how the
outcome connects to compensation and promotion.

It applies to all employees who have completed at least one full cycle. New
joiners inside their first cycle are assessed against their 30-day and 90-day
orientation objectives instead, as described in the new joiner orientation
guide.


2. CADENCE

There are two review cycles per calendar year.

  - The mid-year cycle runs from 1 June to 15 July.
  - The end-of-year cycle runs from 15 November to 31 January.

The end-of-year cycle is the one that carries compensation and promotion
decisions. The mid-year cycle is developmental: it produces feedback and an
adjusted objective set, but no rating that affects pay.

Objectives are agreed at the start of the calendar year and revisited at the
mid-year point. Objectives that have been overtaken by a change in priorities
are rewritten rather than marked as missed.


3. INPUTS TO THE ASSESSMENT

Each cycle draws on three inputs. All three are required; a cycle cannot be
closed with any of them missing.

3.1 Self-assessment

The employee writes a summary of their contribution over the period against
each agreed objective, with concrete evidence, plus a short reflection on what
they want to develop next. This is submitted first, before the manager writes
anything, so that the manager's view is informed by it.

3.2 Manager assessment

The line manager writes an assessment against the same objectives, drawing on
their own observation, the self-assessment and the peer input. The manager is
expected to distinguish between the result achieved and the way it was
achieved; both count.

3.3 Peer feedback

Feedback is collected from a minimum of two colleagues who have worked with the
employee during the period. The employee nominates candidates; the line manager
may add further names to ensure the sample is representative, and confirms the
final list.

Peer feedback is shared with the employee in summarised form. Individual
attribution is not disclosed unless the contributor agrees to it.


4. CALIBRATION

Before outcomes are communicated, line managers within a department meet to
calibrate. The purpose is consistency: the same contribution should produce the
same outcome regardless of which manager wrote the assessment.

Calibration may adjust an outcome. Where it does, the line manager explains the
adjustment and its reasoning to the employee directly -- calibration is never
presented as an anonymous decision from elsewhere.

There is no forced distribution. Managers are not required to place a fixed
percentage of their team into any outcome band.


5. OUTCOME BANDS

  - Exceptional        Contribution well beyond the agreed scope, sustained
                       across the period.
  - Strong             Objectives met, with clear evidence of impact.
  - Solid              Objectives substantially met. This is the expected
                       outcome for most employees in most cycles.
  - Developing         Some objectives not met, or the way of working needs
                       attention. Triggers a written development plan.
  - Not meeting        Sustained shortfall. Triggers a formal improvement plan
                       with People Operations involved.


6. CONNECTION TO COMPENSATION AND PROMOTION

Salary review takes place once a year and is decided in the end-of-year cycle,
with any change effective from 1 April.

Promotion is a separate decision from the rating. It is proposed by the line
manager, evidenced against the target level in the career framework, and
confirmed at department level during calibration. A strong rating does not by
itself imply promotion, and promotion is possible from any band above
Developing.


7. DISAGREEMENT

An employee who disagrees with an outcome should first raise it with their line
manager. If it remains unresolved, they may ask People Operations to review the
process that was followed. The review examines whether the process was applied
correctly; it does not re-perform the assessment.


8. RECORDS

Completed assessments are retained for the period set out in the data
retention schedule and are accessible to the employee, their line manager and
People Operations.
